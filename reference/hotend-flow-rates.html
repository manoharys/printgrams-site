Reading the table

  • Speed = flow ÷ (line width × layer height). Halving the layer height doubles the achievable speed, which is why "600 mm/s" printers advertise with thin layers.
  • PETG manages about 70–80% of the PLA figure, ABS/ASA about 80–90%, TPU 30–50%. Carbon-filled materials are usually limited by the nozzle, not the melt.
  • Larger nozzles raise flow somewhat (a 0.6 mm nozzle typically +20–30%), but the melt zone length is the real limit; that is what high-flow hotends change.

Questions people ask

What is volumetric flow in 3D printing?

The volume of plastic the hotend pushes out per second, in mm³/s. It equals line width × layer height × speed. A 0.42 mm line at 0.2 mm layer height and 100 mm/s is 8.4 mm³/s.

What happens if I exceed the max flow?

The plastic does not fully melt: under-extrusion, gaps between lines, weak layers and a clicking or skipping extruder. Modern slicers (OrcaSlicer, Bambu Studio, PrusaSlicer) cap speed automatically if the filament profile has the right max volumetric speed.

How do I find my own limit?

Print OrcaSlicer's max flowrate test (Calibration → Max flowrate) and note where the surface starts to look rough or gappy. Subtract 10% for a safe setting. Different filaments differ: PETG and TPU melt slower than PLA.
