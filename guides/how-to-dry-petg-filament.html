How to dry PETG filament: temperature, time and signs it's wet

Updated 13 November 2026

Dry PETG at 60–65 °C for 4–6 hours in a filament dryer. A full 1 kg spool needs the full 6 hours. A spool that’s half used dries in about 4. Don’t go above about 70 °C, because PETG starts to soften there and the windings can stick together.

Can you dry PETG at a lower temperature?

Yes, but it’s slow. At 45–50 °C, the limit of many cheap dryers and of printer beds, allow 12–24 hours for a wet spool. A few hours at 45 °C dries the outer layers, and the first metres print well, but the problems come back deeper in the spool.

Signs your PETG is wet

  • Popping or crackling at the nozzle, and small bubbles in the extruded line.
  • Much more stringing than usual, even with the same retraction settings.
  • A rough, slightly foamy surface on parts that are normally glossy.
  • Weak layer bonding, so parts snap along the layers.

PETG picks up moisture faster than PLA. In a room at 50–60% humidity an open spool can absorb enough in 1–3 days to affect print quality.

What to dry it in

Method Works? Notes
Filament dryer (Sunlu, Creality, Eibos, Bambu AMS heating) Yes Easiest. Check that it reaches 60–65 °C, since some only reach 50–55 °C.
Food dehydrator Yes Choose one that holds 65–70 °C and has room for a spool.
Kitchen oven Risky Many ovens overshoot by 10–20 °C at low settings. Use an oven thermometer.
Printer bed under a box Slow Works at 60–70 °C bed temperature if covered, but the heat is uneven.

Check that it’s dry

Weigh the spool before and after drying. A wet 1 kg spool often loses 5–15 g of water. When the weight stops dropping between checks, the spool is dry. The spool remaining calculator has empty-spool weights for 33 brands, so you can also see how much filament is left.

Keep it dry

Store opened spools in a sealed box or bag with silica gel, aiming for under 20% humidity. For long prints, feed straight from a dry box or a heated AMS.
