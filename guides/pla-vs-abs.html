PLA vs ABS: strength, heat, cost per print and which to use

Updated 15 November 2026

Use PLA unless the part will get hot. PLA prints on any printer, barely warps and costs about $20 per kg. ABS holds its shape to about 95 °C, compared with about 55 °C for PLA, and is tougher, but it needs a hot bed, an enclosure and ventilation. Car interiors, parts near motors and lamps, and anything left in the sun are where ABS earns its keep.

Side by side

PLA ABS
Nozzle 190–220 °C 240–270 °C
Bed 50–60 °C 95–110 °C
Enclosure Not needed Recommended (close to essential for big parts)
Softens at ~55 °C ~95 °C
Shrinkage ~0.3% ~0.8%
Density 1.24 g/cm³ 1.04 g/cm³
Metres per kg (1.75 mm) ~335 m ~400 m
Typical price ~$20/kg ~$22/kg
Fumes Mild Styrene, so ventilate

Cost per print: ABS is often slightly cheaper

ABS costs a little more per kilogram, but it is about 16% lighter for the same volume. A spool goes further, and the same model uses fewer grams.

Take a part that is 50 cm³ of plastic:

  • PLA: 50 × 1.24 = 62 g, at 2.0¢ per gram = $1.24
  • ABS: 50 × 1.04 = 52 g, at 2.2¢ per gram = $1.14

So for the same model, ABS filament usually costs about the same or slightly less. The grams to metres converter shows the difference for any spool.

Electricity goes the other way. ABS needs a bed at about 100 °C and a warm enclosure, so the printer draws more. Bambu Lab’s own figures put the A1 at about 95 W for PLA but about 200 W for ABS, and the P1S at 105 W against 140 W. Over a 5-hour print, that adds only a few cents at typical rates. The electricity cost calculator has wattage presets for 37 printers.

The bigger hidden cost is failures. ABS warps and cracks if the printer is open or draughty, and a failed 10-hour print wastes far more than the price difference. If your printer has no enclosure, PLA or PETG is usually cheaper overall.

Strength: it depends what you mean

  • Stiffness: PLA is stiffer. It bends less under load, but snaps when it does fail.
  • Toughness: ABS bends further before breaking and handles knocks better, which suits clips, housings and parts that get dropped.
  • Heat: ABS wins clearly. PLA parts in a hot car can sag within an afternoon.
  • Post-processing: ABS sands well and can be smoothed with acetone vapour. PLA can’t.

When to pick each

Pick PLA for: prototypes, display models, cosplay props, organisers, anything indoors and away from heat, and any printer without an enclosure.

Pick ABS for: car parts, parts near motors or lights, functional housings that get handled roughly, and parts you want to acetone-smooth.

Consider the alternatives: ASA prints like ABS but resists UV, so it is the better choice outdoors. PETG is the middle ground: it holds up to about 70 °C, needs no enclosure and has almost no fumes. PLA+ adds toughness to PLA, but not heat resistance.
