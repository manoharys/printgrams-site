Resin vs filament: which costs more per print?

Updated 10 November 2026

Gram for gram, resin printing costs about 1.5–2.5 times as much as PLA. Standard resin sells for about $25–40 per litre, which is roughly $23–36 per kg at a density of about 1.1 g/ml. Mainstream PLA costs $18–25 per kg. Resin also uses consumables that filament printers don’t.

Extra costs with resin

  • Isopropyl alcohol (IPA) for washing, or a water-washable resin. Budget roughly $0.10–0.30 per print for a small batch.
  • Gloves and paper towels. Nitrile gloves are a must, because uncured resin irritates skin.
  • FEP or nFEP film. It wears out and costs $10–25 per sheet, usually after a few dozen to a few hundred prints.
  • Failed prints. These fill the vat with cured flakes and waste resin, and they happen more often while you’re learning.

Worked examples

Print Resin PLA
28 mm tabletop miniature ~3 ml with supports, ~$0.12 + ~$0.10 consumables Poor detail at this size
Batch of 10 minis on one plate ~35 ml, ~$1.20 + ~$0.30 consumables Not practical
100 g functional bracket ~$3.20–4.00 resin, brittle ~$2.00, tougher

When resin is worth it

Choose resin for miniatures, jewellery masters, dental models and anything small with fine detail. For anything large or that has to take a load, filament is cheaper and stronger.
