How to reduce AMS purge waste on Bambu Lab printers

Updated 10 November 2026

On a Bambu Lab printer with an AMS, every colour change flushes the old filament out of the hotend. A print with hundreds of colour changes can waste more filament than the model itself uses. Bambu Studio and OrcaSlicer show this as “flushed” and “prime tower” in the filament estimate.

Where the waste goes

  • Purge (“poop”). Filament pushed out of the nozzle and dropped down the chute at every colour change, typically 0.1–0.5 g per change.
  • Prime tower. A small tower printed beside the model to stabilise flow after each change.
  • Each filament swap costs a few seconds to tens of seconds, so many swaps also add electricity and print time.

Settings that cut waste

  1. Fewer colour changes. Put single-colour parts on their own plates. Group colours so each layer changes as few times as possible.
  2. Flush into infill. In Bambu Studio and OrcaSlicer, “Flush into objects’ infill” sends some of the purge into the inside of the model.
  3. Flush into a support object. Print a scrap object you can use, or turn on “Flush into this object” for a part whose colour doesn’t matter.
  4. Lower the flushing volumes. The default flushing volumes are cautious. Going from a dark colour to a light one needs the most flushing, while light to dark needs much less. Lower the values a little at a time and watch for colour bleed.
  5. Order the filaments. Put colours that follow each other next to each other, so the printer changes from light to dark where it can.

Count the cost

Add the “flushed” and “prime tower” grams to the model’s weight in the print cost calculator. For a sold print this shows the real material cost. For a typical home print it usually adds a few cents to a dollar. The cost per gram tool shows the price per gram of each spool.
