How to store 3D printer filament so it stays dry

Updated 14 November 2026

Filament absorbs water from the air, and wet filament strings, pops and prints weak parts. Drying fixes it, but storing spools properly means you rarely need to. The rule of thumb is simple: keep opened spools sealed with desiccant, below about 20–30% relative humidity.

How dry each material needs to be

Material Absorbs moisture Store below Open-air time before problems*
PLA Slowly 30% RH Weeks to months
PETG / PCTG Moderately 20–25% RH Days to weeks
ABS / ASA Slowly 30% RH Weeks
TPU Moderately 20% RH Days
Nylon, PVA Fast 10–15% RH Hours to a day

*In a room at 50–60% humidity. Very humid climates shorten these times a lot.

A storage setup that works

  1. An airtight container. A clear plastic storage box with a gasket lid, cereal containers for single spools, or vacuum bags. Cheap boxes without a seal let air in.
  2. Desiccant. Silica gel, about 100–200 g per box. Indicating gel (orange or blue beads that change colour) shows when it is spent.
  3. A hygrometer. A small digital humidity meter in each box tells you when to recharge the desiccant. They are inexpensive and take the guesswork out.
  4. Recharge the desiccant when the box creeps above your target, typically every few weeks. Most silica gel recharges in an oven or dryer at about 100–120 °C; check the packet.

Unopened spools

Factory spools come vacuum-sealed with a desiccant packet and are fine for a long time as long as the bag is intact. Keep the bag if you can: re-sealing a spool in its own bag with fresh desiccant works well for spools you use occasionally.

Printing from storage

For long prints in materials like PETG, TPU and nylon, print straight from a dry box with a PTFE tube to the printer, or from a heated AMS. A spool left on an open holder for a two-day print can pick up enough moisture to show by the end.

When storage was not enough

If a spool starts stringing or popping, dry it, then store it properly. Temperatures and times are in the filament drying chart, with detailed guides for PLA, PETG and TPU. Weighing spools before they go back in the box, with the spool remaining calculator, keeps track of how much you have left too.
