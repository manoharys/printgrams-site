PLA vs PETG: which is cheaper per print?

Updated 10 November 2026

For the same model, PETG costs only slightly more than PLA, usually 0–10% per print. Both sell for about $18–25 per kg from mainstream brands. PETG is a little denser (about 1.27 g/cm³ against 1.24 g/cm³ for PLA), so a part uses about 2% more filament by weight.

Worked example

A part that needs 100 g of PLA needs about 102 g of PETG.

PLA PETG
Density 1.24 g/cm³ 1.27 g/cm³
Weight of the same part 100 g ~102 g
Metres of 1.75 mm filament ~33.5 m ~33.5 m
Material cost at $20/kg $2.00 ~$2.04
Material cost at $20 PLA / $23 PETG $2.00 ~$2.35

Electricity adds a little more for PETG, because the bed runs at 70–85 °C instead of about 60 °C. On a typical Bambu or Prusa printer that’s a few cents for a 5-hour print.

When PETG is the cheaper choice

  • Outdoor or warm places. PLA softens at about 55–60 °C, so a PLA part in a car or in the sun may need reprinting. Reprints cost far more than the price difference.
  • Parts that flex or take impacts. PETG is tougher, so fewer parts crack in use.
  • Sales. Buyers often pay more for PETG, so the margin can be higher. The pricing calculator shows the fees, and how to price 3D prints walks through a full price.

When PLA wins

PLA prints faster and fails less often, especially on open-frame printers. If a failed print wastes the material, a higher failure rate can cost more than the price per kg. Add your own failure rate in the print cost calculator.
