How much does filament shrink? Scale factors by material

Updated 10 November 2026

Most plastics shrink a little as they cool. For parts that have to fit something, like press fits, threads or holes for bearings, scale the model up slightly in your slicer to make up for it.

Typical shrinkage by material

Material Typical shrinkage Scale factor to try
PLA 0.2–0.3% 100.2–100.3%
PETG 0.3–0.4% 100.3–100.4%
ABS 0.5–0.8% 100.5–100.8%
ASA 0.5–0.7% 100.5–100.7%
Nylon (PA) 1–2% 101–102%
PA-CF / PET-CF 0.2–0.5% 100.2–100.5%

These are starting points. The real figure depends on the brand, the colour, the enclosure temperature and the shape of the part.

Measure your own

  1. Print a 100 mm calibration bar or cube, at least 50 mm along each axis, with 3 or more walls.
  2. Let it cool to room temperature for an hour.
  3. Measure with digital calipers.
  4. Enter the designed and measured sizes into the shrinkage scale calculator. It gives the slicer scale factor for X/Y and Z separately, because they often differ.

Shrinkage or flow?

If walls come out too thick and holes too small on every material, the problem is probably over-extrusion rather than shrinkage. Calibrate the flow first with the flow rate calculator, then check shrinkage.
