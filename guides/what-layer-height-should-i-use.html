What layer height should I use? A practical guide for 0.4 mm nozzles

Updated 10 November 2026

With a standard 0.4 mm nozzle, use 0.2 mm for everyday prints, 0.12–0.16 mm for figures and fine detail, and 0.28 mm for large functional parts where speed matters more than looks.

The 25–75% rule

Layer height should be between 25% and 75% of the nozzle diameter. Below that, the plastic barely gets pushed out and the printer can’t position it accurately. Above it, layers bond poorly.

Nozzle Minimum Typical Maximum
0.2 mm 0.05 mm 0.10 mm 0.15 mm
0.4 mm 0.10 mm 0.20 mm 0.30 mm
0.6 mm 0.15 mm 0.30 mm 0.45 mm
0.8 mm 0.20 mm 0.40 mm 0.60 mm

How layer height changes print time

Print time roughly scales with the number of layers. Halving the layer height from 0.2 mm to 0.1 mm about doubles the time. Going from 0.2 mm to 0.28 mm saves about a quarter. The print time estimator shows the effect on your own part.

Magic numbers

Many printers move the Z axis with a stepper motor on a leadscrew. One full step of the motor moves Z by a fixed amount, for example 0.04 mm on a printer with an 8 mm lead screw and a 1.8° motor. Layer heights that are a whole number of full steps (0.12, 0.16, 0.20, 0.24, 0.28 mm on that printer) give more consistent layers than in-between values like 0.15 mm.

The magic layer heights calculator works out the values for your printer. Each printer page also lists them.
