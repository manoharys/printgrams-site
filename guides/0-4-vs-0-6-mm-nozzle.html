0.4 mm vs 0.6 mm nozzle: speed, strength and detail

Updated 11 November 2026

Most printers ship with a 0.4 mm nozzle. A 0.6 mm nozzle pushes out about 2.25 times as much plastic per millimetre of travel at the same layer height, because the line is wider and the area goes up with the square. In practice, a 0.6 mm nozzle prints functional parts about 30–50% faster, with stronger walls, but less detail.

0.4 mm 0.6 mm
Typical line width 0.42–0.45 mm 0.62–0.68 mm
Layer height range 0.10–0.30 mm 0.15–0.45 mm
Everyday layer height 0.20 mm 0.30 mm
Fine detail and small text Good Fair
Strength of the same part Baseline Higher (thicker walls, better layer bonding)
Clogs with CF or glow-in-the-dark filament More often Less often

When to use 0.6 mm

  • Brackets, mounts, enclosures, tool holders and other parts where function matters more than looks.
  • Large prints such as helmets, storage boxes and planters.
  • Abrasive filaments (CF, GF, glow-in-the-dark). Use a hardened steel nozzle for these.

When to stay on 0.4 mm

  • Miniatures, figures and anything with lettering smaller than about 5 mm.
  • Thin walls under about 1 mm.

What to change after switching

  1. Select the 0.6 mm nozzle profile in your slicer. Bambu Studio, OrcaSlicer and PrusaSlicer all have one.
  2. Use a layer height between 0.15 and 0.45 mm. The magic layer heights calculator lists the clean values for your printer.
  3. Check that the hotend can melt enough plastic for the speed you want. Higher flow can be the limit, so see the hotend flow rate reference.
  4. Recalibrate flow with the flow rate calculator.
