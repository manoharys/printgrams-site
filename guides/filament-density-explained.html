Filament density: why 1 kg of ABS prints more than 1 kg of PETG

Updated 9 November 2026

Your slicer calculates the volume of plastic it will extrude. To show grams, it multiplies by the density set in the filament profile. If that density is wrong, the gram figure is wrong — and so is any cost estimate built on it.

Density of common filaments

Material g/cm³ Metres per kg (1.75 mm)
PP 0.90 462
Nylon PA12 1.01 412
ABS / HIPS 1.04 400
ASA 1.07 389
Nylon PA6 1.14 365
TPU 95A 1.21 344
PLA 1.24 335
PETG 1.27 327
Matte PLA 1.30 320

A kilogram of ABS is almost 400 m of filament; a kilogram of PETG is 327 m. For the same part, ABS uses 18% fewer grams. Compare spools per metre, not per kilogram — the cost per gram and metre tool does this.

Why the figure on the spool matters

Filled filaments vary a lot. “Wood PLA” ranges from 0.8 g/cm³ (foamed) to 1.4; carbon-fibre blends are 1.26–1.30; matte PLA with mineral filler is around 1.30. Metal-filled PLA can exceed 3 g/cm³, so a 500 g spool is a very short length. Always use the manufacturer’s technical data sheet figure in your slicer profile.

Measuring density yourself

Cut exactly 1 m of filament, weigh it on a 0.01 g scale, and divide by 2.405 (the volume in cm³ of one metre of 1.75 mm filament; 6.38 cm³ for 2.85 mm). Example: 2.98 g ÷ 2.405 = 1.24 g/cm³. The length ↔ weight converter will do the arithmetic with a custom density.
