How much does it cost to 3D print a cosplay helmet?

Updated 12 November 2026

A full-size cosplay helmet usually takes 400–900 g of PLA and 25–60 hours of printing. That works out to about $10–25 in filament and electricity. Finishing materials add another $15–40 the first time, but you can reuse most of them.

Typical breakdown

Item Approx. cost
Filament, 600 g PLA at $20/kg $12.00
Electricity, 40 h × ~0.1 kWh at $0.17/kWh $0.70
Printer wear $1–3
One failed part (budget 10%) $1.20
Print total about $15
Filler primer, sandpaper, paint, clear coat (first time) $15–40

How to cut the cost and time

  • Use thin walls and little infill. Helmets are shells. 2–3 walls and 0–5% infill are usually enough, and the shell is often stronger than people expect.
  • Use a 0.6 mm nozzle. It prints large parts faster with thicker lines and hides layer lines once sanded. See 0.4 vs 0.6 mm nozzles.
  • Split the model to fit your printer. Most helmets need 2–6 pieces on a 256 mm printer. Fewer seams mean less filling and sanding.
  • Scale carefully. Measure your head circumference and scale the model before printing. Reprinting a 300 g piece costs more than checking first. The STL analyzer shows the real size and estimated weight.
