Filament diameter tolerance: why 1.70 mm vs 1.75 mm matters

Updated 11 November 2026

Slicers assume your filament is exactly 1.75 mm across. If it isn’t, every move pushes out the wrong amount of plastic. Because the volume depends on the square of the diameter, small differences add up quickly:

Measured diameter Plastic per mm vs 1.75 mm
1.70 mm −5.6% (under-extrusion)
1.72 mm −3.4%
1.73 mm −2.3%
1.75 mm 0%
1.77 mm +2.3%
1.80 mm +5.8% (over-extrusion)

What tolerance to expect

Most brands advertise ±0.02 mm or ±0.03 mm. Budget brands may say ±0.05 mm. Within ±0.02 mm, the error is under 2.3%, which you won’t see on most prints.

How to measure

  1. Use digital calipers. A cheap pair is fine at this accuracy.
  2. Measure in 5–10 places over a metre or two of filament.
  3. At each spot, measure twice at 90° to each other, because filament can be slightly oval.
  4. Average all the readings.

When to change your slicer

If the average is more than about 0.02 mm away from 1.75 mm, enter the measured value as the filament diameter in your slicer’s filament settings. Then run a flow test and enter the results in the flow rate calculator, which corrects for any remaining difference.

The length and weight converter accepts any diameter, so you can also check how many metres a spool really holds.
