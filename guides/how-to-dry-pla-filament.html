How to dry PLA filament: temperature, time and whether you need to

Updated 13 November 2026

Dry PLA at 45–50 °C for 4–6 hours. Don’t go above about 55 °C. PLA softens around 55–60 °C, and a hotter dryer can fuse the windings together or warp the spool.

Does PLA even need drying?

Usually not. A fresh, sealed spool is dry enough to print. PLA absorbs moisture much more slowly than PETG, TPU or nylon, so drying only matters if:

  • the spool has been open for weeks or months in a humid room (above about 50% humidity);
  • it’s a silk, matte or wood-filled PLA, which tend to be more sensitive;
  • it shows the signs below.

Signs your PLA is wet

  • Fine stringing and wisps that weren’t there when the spool was new.
  • Crackling or popping at the nozzle.
  • A rougher, less glossy surface, and blobs on the outer walls.
  • Filament that snaps when you bend it. Old PLA can become brittle, and drying restores some of its flexibility.

If none of these appear, look at print settings (temperature, retraction, cooling) before drying.

What to dry it in

Method Safe for PLA? Notes
Filament dryer Yes Set 45–50 °C. Most dryers have a PLA preset.
Heated AMS or dry box Yes Lower temperatures take longer: allow 8–12 h at 40–45 °C.
Food dehydrator Yes Check the actual temperature with a thermometer.
Kitchen oven Not recommended Lowest settings often overshoot past 60 °C and soften the spool.
Printer bed under a box Works 50–55 °C bed, spool flat, cardboard box over it, turn halfway through.

How long is long enough?

Weigh the spool before and after. A damp 1 kg PLA spool usually loses only 2–5 g, much less than PETG or nylon. When the weight stops changing, it’s dry. The spool remaining calculator knows empty-spool weights by brand, so the same weighing also tells you how much filament is left.

Keep it dry

A sealed bin with a few packs of silica gel is enough for PLA. Aim for under 30% humidity. Rechargeable desiccant with a colour indicator tells you when to re-dry the gel.

For other materials, see the filament drying chart, how to dry PETG and the PLA material page.
