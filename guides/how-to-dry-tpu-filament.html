How to dry TPU filament: temperature, time and signs it's wet

Updated 14 November 2026

Dry TPU at 50–70 °C for 6–8 hours. Most 95A spools are happy at 60–70 °C for about 6 hours. If your dryer only reaches 50–55 °C, allow 8 hours or more. Softer grades and some brands specify lower temperatures, so check the datasheet when you have one.

Does TPU need drying?

Usually, yes. TPU is moderately hygroscopic: it picks up water faster than PLA and about as fast as PETG. Because TPU is printed slowly, the filament spends a long time in the hot zone, so any moisture has plenty of time to turn to steam. Many people dry every TPU spool before a print, even a new one.

Signs your TPU is wet

  • Heavy stringing, with fine hairs across the whole part even when retraction is low or off.
  • Popping or hissing at the nozzle and visible bubbles in the extruded line.
  • Rough, foamy walls and small holes on the surface.
  • Weak, crumbly layers: parts tear along the layer lines instead of stretching.

Some stringing is normal with TPU. If it stays the same after drying, look at temperature (try 5–10 °C lower) and travel moves, not moisture.

What to dry it in

Method Works? Notes
Filament dryer Yes The easiest option. Most dryers have a TPU preset of 50–65 °C.
Food dehydrator Yes Check the real temperature with a thermometer.
Printer bed under a box Slow Possible at 60–70 °C, but heat is uneven, so turn the spool halfway through.
Kitchen oven Risky Low settings often overshoot. TPU tolerates heat better than PLA, but the spool may not.

Print it dry

TPU prints slowly, often for many hours, and can pick up moisture during a long print in a humid room. Feed it straight from the dryer or a sealed dry box with a PTFE tube to the extruder. TPU does not feed reliably through most AMS-style multi-material systems, so a dry box next to the printer is the usual setup.

Check that it’s dry

Weigh the spool before and after drying. When the weight stops dropping between checks, it is dry. The spool remaining calculator has empty-spool weights by brand, so the same weighing tells you how much is left.

For other materials, see the filament drying chart, how to dry PETG and how to dry PLA. The TPU 95A material page has print settings.
