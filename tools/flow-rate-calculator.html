How it's calculated

new e-steps          = current × requested / actually extruded
new rotation distance = current × actually extruded / requested
new flow %            = current × target wall / measured wall
volumetric flow       = line width × layer height × speed (mm³/s)
  • E-steps (Marlin) and rotation distance (Klipper) are inverses of each other: more steps per mm means a smaller rotation distance.
  • Flow calibrates the extrusion multiplier so a single wall measures what the slicer intended. Measure in several places with calipers and average.
  • Volumetric flow is the ceiling: if width × height × speed exceeds what the hotend can melt, the slicer silently slows down or you under-extrude.

Questions people ask

How do I calibrate e-steps?

Mark the filament 120 mm above the extruder entry, command 100 mm of extrusion at 1–2 mm/s, then measure what is left to the mark. New e-steps = old × 100 / (120 − remaining). Save with M500.

What is a good max volumetric flow?

A stock Ender 3 hotend manages about 9 mm³/s on PLA; Prusa MK4S about 16 (22 with the high-flow nozzle); Bambu P1/X1 about 26 on PLA (Bambu rates them at 32 with ABS); high-flow hotends such as the Rapido UHF or FlowTech reach 30–35 on PLA.
