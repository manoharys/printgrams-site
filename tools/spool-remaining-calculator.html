How it's calculated

by weight:   remaining = gross weight − empty spool weight
by diameter: volume = π × (outer² − core²) / 4 × width × packing factor
             remaining grams = volume (cm³) × density
  • Weighing is exact if you know the empty spool weight. We list the common ones; print the spool brand's weight on a label for next time.
  • Measuring works without a scale: the filament forms a hollow cylinder between the hub and the outer layer. Random winding leaves gaps, so a packing factor (about 0.7) corrects for air.
  • The “will my print finish?” check compares the remaining grams with what your slicer needs, plus a safety margin for purge and waste.

Questions people ask

How much does an empty Bambu Lab spool weigh?

The bare reusable plastic spool weighs about 213 g. Filament that ships on it is wound on a 40 g cardboard core, so a factory spool reads about 255 g once empty and about 1,255 g when full. A refill core alone is about 40 g.

How much does an empty filament spool weigh in general?

Plastic spools: roughly 125–250 g (older black spools 200–250 g, modern thin ones 125–160 g). Cardboard spools: 125–175 g. Check the brand list in the tool for exact figures.

How accurate is the diameter method?

Within about ±15%, because winding density varies. It is good enough to decide whether to start a 200 g print on a spool that looks half full.

How much do empty Prusament, eSUN, Sunlu and Polymaker spools weigh?

Prusament 1 kg: about 194 g. eSUN: about 230 g on the classic plastic spool, about 160 g on the newer cardboard one. Sunlu current plastic: about 130 g. Polymaker cardboard: about 140 g. Elegoo cardboard: about 154 g. The empty spool weights table lists 30+ spools with measured ranges.

How do I convert grams left into metres?

For 1.75 mm PLA, multiply grams by about 0.335 (1 kg ≈ 335 m). PETG is slightly denser (about 0.33 m per gram), TPU and ABS lighter. The calculator does this automatically for the material you choose.

Should I start a print if the spool is nearly empty?

Only if the remaining filament is at least 10–15% more than the slicer estimate. Purge lines, skirts, brims and multi-colour purges eat into the margin, and running out mid-print usually ruins the part unless your printer has a run-out sensor.
