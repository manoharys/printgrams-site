How it's calculated

cost per gram  = spool price / net grams
cost per metre = cost per gram × grams per metre (from density and diameter)
  • Net weight is the filament only — many “1 kg” spools are 1 kg of filament, but budget brands sometimes list gross weight or ship 750 g or 500 g spools.
  • Cost per metre lets you compare materials with different densities: ABS is lighter than PETG, so a kilogram goes further.

Questions people ask

How much does filament cost per gram?

Budget PLA at $15–20/kg is 1.5–2 cents per gram; PETG about 2–2.5 cents; TPU 3–4 cents; carbon-fibre nylon 6–10 cents per gram.

What does 3D printing cost per gram, all in?

For PLA on a typical home printer, about 2–3 cents per gram once electricity, printer wear and a 10% failure allowance are added to the filament. Electricity adds only a fraction of a cent per gram (a 100 W printer extruding about 12 g per hour uses roughly 0.15¢ of power per gram at US rates). Print services charge far more, often 10–50 cents per gram, because their price includes labour, overheads and margin. The print cost calculator gives the full figure for your own print.

Is a cheaper spool always cheaper per gram?

No. Compare net weight: a $19 spool with 750 g of filament costs 2.5¢ per gram, more than a $24 spool with 1 kg (2.4¢). Also watch for "1 kg" listings that include the spool weight.

Why compare cost per metre instead of per gram?

Prints use volume, not weight. Lighter materials like ABS (1.04 g/cm³) give about 19% more metres per kilogram than PLA (1.24 g/cm³), so a slightly pricier ABS spool can cost the same per print.
