How it's calculated

material     = grams / 1000 × price per kg
electricity  = watts × hours / 1000 × rate per kWh
depreciation = printer price / lifetime hours × hours
labour       = minutes / 60 × hourly rate
subtotal     = material + electricity + depreciation + maintenance + labour + post-processing + consumables
true cost    = subtotal / (1 − failure rate)
price        = true cost × (1 + markup)
  • Filament is priced pro-rata from the spool price. If you only know the length, pick the material and we convert metres to grams using its density.
  • Electricity uses the printer's average draw while printing. Pick your printer for a measured figure, or type your own.
  • Depreciation spreads the printer price over its useful life (5,000 hours by default, roughly three years of regular use).
  • The failure-rate allowance is the part most calculators skip: if 1 in 10 prints fails, each good print really costs 1/0.9 of its nominal cost.

Questions people ask

How much does it cost to 3D print something?

For a typical 50 g PLA part printed in 4 hours, filament is about $1.00 (at $20/kg) and electricity about $0.07, so under $1.10 before wear and labour. Larger, slower or exotic-material prints scale from there — the calculator above gives the exact number for your inputs.

Where do I find grams and hours?

Every slicer shows them after slicing: Bambu Studio and OrcaSlicer on the right-hand panel under “Filament” and “Total time”; PrusaSlicer in the Sliced Info box; Cura at the bottom-right of the preview.

What markup should I use for selling prints?

Hobby sellers typically use 100–300% on the true cost; businesses price on value and the market instead. Use the marketplace pricing tool to add Etsy or eBay fees on top.

Should I include the printer price?

If you sell prints, yes — a $600 printer run for 5,000 hours costs $0.12 per hour before any maintenance. For personal use most people ignore it.
